A **digital garden** is a different way of thinking about personal publishing. Unlike a blog — where posts are dated, polished, and shipped — a garden is a living space. Notes here exist in various states of growth.

## The blog vs. the garden

A blog is chronological. It implies that newer = better. Each post is a finished product that stands alone.

A garden is topological. Notes connect to other notes. Old notes get updated. Ideas evolve. There's no pressure to have a hot take on whatever happened this week.

> "The stream makes you complicit in its own fleetingness." — Mike Caulfield

## How things connect here

Every note can link to another using `[[double brackets]]`. This creates a web of connections that you can explore visually in the [[Graph View]] page.

Over time, the graph becomes a map of how you actually think — which ideas keep coming up, what things connect to what.

## Stages of growth

I loosely categorize notes by how developed they are:

- **Seedling** — a raw idea, barely formed
- **Budding** — has some structure, needs more thought
- **Evergreen** — well-developed, stable, and useful

Not everything here is evergreen. Some notes will stay seedlings forever.

## What this garden is for

This is a place to think out loud about [[Interesting Ideas and Projects]] — things I've been building, reading, or obsessing over. It's not optimized for an audience; it's optimized for me. But if something here is useful to you, that's a bonus.
