There's a trap that most writers fall into: waiting until an idea is fully formed before sharing it.

The problem is that ideas rarely become fully formed in isolation. They need friction — other people's reactions, questions, and contradictions — to sharpen.

## The cost of perfectionism

When you wait until something is perfect:

- You get less feedback, earlier
- The idea stays in your head longer and starts to feel untouchable
- You end up publishing less

Perfectionism compounds. The longer you wait, the more polished it *needs* to be to justify the wait.

## Thinking in public

Writing in a digital garden sidesteps this. A note doesn't need to be done. It can be a question, a hunch, an early observation.

```
First draft: "I think X."
After feedback: "X works because of Y, but fails when Z."
Final understanding: "X, Y, and Z are actually the same thing."
```

That progression only happens if you share the first draft.

## What to share

Not everything — but more than you think. A good heuristic: if you've thought about something for more than an hour, it's probably worth writing down. If you'd explain it to a friend, you can write it in the garden.
