Most learning happens in private. You read, you struggle, you figure something out — and then you either move on or maybe write a polished article after the fact.

Learning in public is different. You **share the process** while it's happening.

## What it actually looks like

- A thread about something you're trying to figure out, even before you have the answer
- A short note about a concept you *thought* you understood but didn't
- A blog post titled "Here's what I got wrong"
- A project built in the open, with a README that documents the confusion as much as the solution

This is the philosophy behind [[On Writing in Public]] and, more broadly, behind this whole garden.

## Why it works

**Accountability.** When you say "I'm learning X", you're more likely to follow through.

**Clarification.** Explaining something to others forces you to find the gaps in your own understanding. The [Feynman Technique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feynman_Technique) works because teaching reveals what you don't actually know.

**Compounding.** Public learning creates a trail. Three months later, you'll find your own old posts when searching. Two years later, someone else will.

**Serendipity.** Someone who knows more will correct you. Someone who knows less will thank you. Neither would have happened if you'd kept it private.

## The fear

The main fear is looking dumb. But:

1. Most people are too busy thinking about themselves to judge you
2. Being wrong in public and then correcting yourself is more credible than never being wrong at all
3. The people worth impressing respect intellectual honesty

## A practical start

Pick one thing you're learning this week. Write one sentence about what you understood, and one sentence about what confused you. Post it somewhere. That's it.

The bar is lower than you think. [[Evergreen Notes]] can start as seedlings.
